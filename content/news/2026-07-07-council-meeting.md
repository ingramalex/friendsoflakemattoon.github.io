# July 7, 2026 Mattoon City Council: Septic Inspections, Bowfishing, and a Watershed Planning Meeting

## What residents raised

A resident opened public comment with concerns about older or non-compliant septic systems around Lake Paradise and Lake Mattoon, and whether effluent from grandfathered or uninspected systems is reaching the water ([03:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IkW22GlJH3Y&t=221s), [04:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IkW22GlJH3Y&t=255s)).

A second resident — the captions render the name as Roy Birch — asked the City to revise its ordinances to permit bowfishing for invasive carp on Lake Mattoon, so that City rules line up with Illinois state law. His stated reasoning was that invasive carp stir up nutrients from the lakebed, contributing to harmful algal blooms, and that they affect native fish populations ([06:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IkW22GlJH3Y&t=385s)).

## What officials committed to

City staff said they are working with the Coles County Health Department to begin inspecting private sewage systems around Lake Paradise, starting a few at a time ([04:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IkW22GlJH3Y&t=255s)).

On bowfishing, officials took the resident's contact information and committed to reviewing the existing City ordinance against state regulations ([07:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IkW22GlJH3Y&t=478s)).

## Watershed planning meeting announced

A commissioner — captioned as Clawson — announced a public watershed planning meeting covering both Lake Paradise and Lake Mattoon, to be held at the water treatment plant. The stated purpose is to review previous findings and set goals and initiatives for long-term water supply protection ([17:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IkW22GlJH3Y&t=1063s)). Anyone planning to attend should confirm the date and time directly with the City, as the captions are not a reliable source for scheduling details.

## Algae response

A commissioner — captioned as Butler — asked what proactive steps are underway to prevent blue-green algal blooms. Staff reported that powdered activated carbon dosing has begun at treatment, testing is active, and algaecide treatments were recently applied on Lake Mattoon ([18:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IkW22GlJH3Y&t=1111s)).

## Who regulates septic systems

Council members discussed jurisdiction over septic systems near the lakes, with the discussion landing on public health departments rather than the EPA holding that authority. Staff said inspections would start in Coles County before coordinating with Cumberland and Shelby counties ([20:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IkW22GlJH3Y&t=1215s)). That sequencing matters for many Lake Mattoon property owners, whose parcels fall outside Coles County and outside Mattoon city limits.

No votes were recorded on any of the lake-related items above.
