# Oct. 19, 2026: Council Meets Tuesday, and the Watershed Committee Has Gone Four Months Without Posting a Date

## Coming up

The **Mattoon City Council meets Tuesday, Oct. 20**, its regular 1st-and-3rd-Tuesday slot. The agenda and packet are posted as [downloads](https://mattoon.illinois.gov/download/city-council-meeting-2026-10-20/), but both PDFs sit behind a CAPTCHA, so we could read only the document titles — not whether anything on the lake is on the list. If you want to know before you go, you'll have to open them yourself from the [meetings index](https://mattoon.illinois.gov/government/citycouncil/upcomingmeetings/).

Worth repeating for the many of us who live on the lake but outside Mattoon city limits: we don't vote for this council, and it governs the lake anyway. The meetings are open, and the recordings go up on the city's [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_2ux8VDItcI) — the Oct. 6 meeting is already posted.

No lake business turned up in the six most recent documents checked from the [Shelby County Board](https://shelbycounty-il.gov/cominutes.aspx), the [Cumberland County Board](https://cumberlandcoil.gov/county-board-agendas-minutes/), or the [City of Neoga](https://neoga.org/city-council/). On the [Coles County](https://www.colesco.illinois.gov/board/agendas/) side, Lake Mattoon appears only as a passing reference in the county's [hazard mitigation plan](https://www.colesco.illinois.gov/static/plans/Coles%20County%20HMP%207.8.2025.pdf) and [2026 comprehensive plan](https://www.colesco.illinois.gov/static/plans/Coles%20County%202026%20Comprehensive%20Plan.pdf) — nothing pending.

## Water quality

Four new treated-water results since our last brief, all from the city's [Sept. 23 update](https://mattoon.illinois.gov/public-notices/water-quality-update-september-23-2026/):

- Aug. 10 — 0.009 ppb
- Aug. 24 — 0.053 ppb
- Sept. 7 — 0.00 ppb
- Sept. 21 — 0.06 ppb

These are finished tap water, not lake water. The sensitive-population advisory level is 0.30 ppb and the general advisory level is 1.6 ppb. Across 79 samples on record, the highest single reading has been 0.14 ppb and there have been no exceedances. Nothing in this run has moved out of the range we've been seeing all summer.

Two notes on the city's wording. First, its notices describe the substance as a "harmful algal bloom neurological toxin." Microcystin is a liver toxin, not a neurological one; the numbers are what matter, but the label is wrong and we'll flag it this once. Second, the notices call results of 0.053 and 0.071 ppb "non-detectable levels" while printing a number above zero — read the figures, not the adjective.

No newer notice has been posted since Sept. 23.

## Watershed planning

Nothing new. The Coles County SWCD's [committee page](http://www.colescountyswcd.org/resources/watersheds/lake-mattoon-lake-paradise-watershed-committee/) was last modified July 1 and still advertises a July 8 meeting at the Mattoon Water Treatment Plant as the next one. That meeting's [agenda](http://www.colescountyswcd.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026.7.8-MP-Watershed-Meeting-Agenda.docx) listed setting a recurring schedule — possibly the first Wednesday of odd-numbered months — as an open question. No result has been posted. The committee is open to anyone, in the watershed or not: **mattoonandparadisewatershed@gmail.com**. We have no evidence this week on TMDL work, funding, or erosion projects.
