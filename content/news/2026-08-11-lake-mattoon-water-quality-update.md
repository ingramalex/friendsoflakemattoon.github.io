# Where Lake Mattoon Stands: Water Quality, the Wake Boat Ban, and What's Next

*August 11, 2026*

If you live around Lake Mattoon, you have probably heard two things in the last year: that the water was unsafe to drink, and that wake boats are being banned. Both stories are real, but they are not the same story — and the difference matters.

## First, a distinction worth getting right

Lake Mattoon sits roughly 12 miles southwest of the City of Mattoon, spanning parts of **Coles, Shelby, and Cumberland counties**. The lake is *owned* by the City of Mattoon and serves as a public water source, but the lake itself — and most of the people who live on it — are outside city limits.

That geography explains a lot of the frustration residents feel. Decisions about the lake are made at Mattoon City Council meetings, by officials that most lakefront residents do not vote for.

It also clears up a common misunderstanding about the 2025 water crisis. According to the City's own [Water Quality Response and Recovery Report](https://mattoon.illinois.gov/wp-content/uploads/Water-Quality-Response-and-Recovery-Report.pdf), **Lake Paradise is Mattoon's primary drinking water source. Lake Mattoon is a contingency source, and no drinking water was drawn from it during the July 2025 events.** The algal bloom that shut off the taps was in Lake Paradise.

## What happened in July 2025

On July 9, 2025, the Illinois EPA sampled Mattoon's water treatment plant and Lake Paradise. The next day, the City was notified that **microcystin** — a toxin produced by blue-green algae — measured 0.35 parts per billion, above the 0.3 ppb EPA advisory threshold. A Do Not Drink order followed.

The order was lifted July 13, **reissued July 15** when levels rose again to 0.385 ppb, and finally lifted July 18 after three consecutive days below the threshold. The City ran water distribution sites at Lytle Park and Williams Elementary, delivered water to residents without transportation, and increased carbon dosing at the treatment plant.

One point of context the City's notices tend to blur: microcystin is primarily a **liver** toxin, not a neurological one, despite the "neurological toxin" phrasing used in the recurring updates. The 0.3 ppb advisory applies to sensitive groups — children under 6, pregnant and nursing women, people with liver conditions, dialysis patients, the elderly, and immunocompromised people. For healthy adults and older children the advisory level is 1.6 ppb, and for swimming it is 8.0 µg/L.

## Where the numbers stand today

The City has posted a running log of test results since the crisis. Reviewing **all 75 published readings** from July 2025 through July 2026:

- **Every single reading has been below the 0.3 ppb advisory level.**
- The highest post-crisis reading was **0.14 ppb** (July 21, 2025).
- The most recent sample, collected **July 26, 2026, measured 0.071 ppb**.
- Testing runs **every two weeks**, per Illinois EPA recommendation.

In plain terms: treated drinking water has stayed within advisory limits for a full year. That is genuinely good news, and it is worth saying plainly.

## The fight that is actually live: wake boats

In April 2026, the Mattoon City Council voted **3–2** to restrict wake surfing on Lake Mattoon. For the 2026 season, wake surfing must occur at least **600 feet from shore in water at least 15 feet deep**. Beginning in **2027, wake surfing is banned outright**.

The City's stated rationale is water quality: larger wakes accelerate shoreline erosion, which washes soil and fertilizer nutrients into the lake — and nutrient loading is what feeds algal blooms in the first place.

Many lakefront homeowners disagree, arguing erosion is modest and driven mostly by wind and weather. The decision followed an advisory committee that held public input meetings in February and March 2026, including one at Lakeside Timbers RV Campground in Neoga.

## What we are still waiting on

The City's recovery report is candid about the response, but two of its sections — **"Lessons Learned"** and **"Next Steps and Recommendations"** — were published blank, marked "To Be Added after Debrief." A year later, we have not found a published version of that debrief.

Three long-term fixes were floated in the report and have not been publicly resolved:

1. **Lake aeration systems**
2. **Ozone treatment upgrades** at the water treatment plant
3. **Algaecide application** to both Lake Mattoon and Lake Paradise

A **Lake Mattoon and Lake Paradise Watershed Committee** was also formed in response to the blooms. Its current roster, meeting schedule, and minutes are not posted on the City website.

## Why this page exists

Most of the information above is public — but it is scattered across public notices, a 37-page PDF, council packets, and local news coverage. Friends of Lake Mattoon is working to centralize it so residents can see what is being discussed, and show up when it matters.

Mattoon City Council meets the **1st and 3rd Tuesdays** of each month. Agendas are posted at [mattoon.illinois.gov](https://mattoon.illinois.gov/government/citycouncil/upcomingmeetings/).
