# Council Meets Tuesday Oct. 6 — Agenda Locked Behind a CAPTCHA; Tap Readings Hold Near Zero

## Coming up

The **Mattoon City Council meets Tuesday, October 6**. The [meeting page](https://mattoon.illinois.gov/download/city-council-meeting-2026-10-06/) lists an agenda and a packet, but both PDFs sit behind a CAPTCHA, so we could read only the document titles — not what is on them. If you want to know whether anything touching the lake is up for a vote, you will have to open them yourself or attend. The council meets on the first and third Tuesdays; the [upcoming meetings index](https://mattoon.illinois.gov/government/citycouncil/upcomingmeetings/) is the place to watch.

Worth repeating for most of our readers: the council that governs Lake Mattoon is elected by people inside Mattoon city limits. If you live on the lake, you likely cannot vote for them, but the meetings are open and public comment is open to you.

No Lake Mattoon business appeared in the six most recent documents checked from the [Shelby County Board](https://shelbycounty-il.gov/cominutes.aspx), the [Cumberland County Board](https://cumberlandcoil.gov/county-board-agendas-minutes/), or the [City of Neoga](https://neoga.org/city-council/). At the [Coles County Board](https://www.colesco.illinois.gov/board/agendas/), the lake appears only as background reference in the [hazard mitigation plan](https://www.colesco.illinois.gov/static/plans/Coles%20County%20HMP%207.8.2025.pdf) and the [2026 comprehensive plan](https://www.colesco.illinois.gov/static/plans/Coles%20County%202026%20Comprehensive%20Plan.pdf) — nothing pending.

## Water quality

Four new results since our last brief, all from the City's [September 23 notice](https://mattoon.illinois.gov/public-notices/water-quality-update-september-23-2026/):

- Aug. 10 — 0.009 ppb
- Aug. 24 — 0.053 ppb
- Sept. 7 — 0.00 ppb
- Sept. 21 — 0.06 ppb

The sensitive-population advisory level is 0.3 ppb; the general advisory level is 1.6 ppb. Across 79 samples in this series the highest result has been 0.14 ppb, and there have been no exceedances. Nothing in these four readings changes that picture.

Two things to keep straight. First, these are samples of **finished treated tap water** from the City system — they are not measurements of the lake itself, and they say nothing about private wells. Second, the City's notices describe the substance as a "harmful algal bloom neurological toxin." Microcystin is a liver toxin, not a neurological one. We flag the discrepancy once and will not repeat it; the numbers themselves are what they are.

For context that bears repeating: Lake Paradise is the City's primary drinking water source and Lake Mattoon is a contingency source. The July 2025 Do Not Drink order followed a bloom in Lake Paradise.
