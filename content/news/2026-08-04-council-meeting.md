# Mattoon City Council, August 4, 2026: Drainage Project Bids Approved After Downstream Flooding Concerns

## What a resident raised

A written comment from a resident — the captions render the name as Diane Lathrop — was read into the record ([40:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PV7CejNeCpo&t=2407s)). The concern was that moving stormwater faster through the new 60-inch storm sewer pipe planned for the Little Wabash Drainage Project would worsen flooding on downstream Lake Road properties, and would carry more chemical runoff into the watershed that feeds the city's water supply.

That is the kind of comment worth listening to directly, since the captions are unreliable on names and numbers.

## What the city committed to

The city manager responded that Phase 3 of the drainage project includes detention basins — the captions place them behind Bimbo Bakeries and near Circle Drive — intended to slow storm runoff before it moves downstream.

He also committed to meeting with the resident to walk through the project plans and look at whether change orders or design amendments are warranted ([39:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PV7CejNeCpo&t=2362s)). That commitment is listed in the findings as proposed, not scheduled. Residents downstream may want to follow up on whether the meeting happens and what comes of it.

## The votes

**Little Wabash Drainage Project, Phase 1 piping** ([35:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PV7CejNeCpo&t=2129s)) — The council approved the plans and specifications and authorized the city to solicit bids. The vote passed. Discussion covered the downstream flood risk along Little Wabash Creek and the potential runoff effects on watershed water quality.

Note that authorizing bids is not the same as awarding a contract. There should be a later meeting where bids come back to the council, which is a second point of entry for anyone who wants to comment.

**Water treatment plant equipment** ([33:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PV7CejNeCpo&t=2004s)) — The council approved a proposal, given in the captions as $74,868 from a firm rendered as Hydro-Kinetics, to replace five modulating electric actuators on Filter #2 at the water treatment plant. Staff described the existing units as 25 years old and said the replacement keeps equipment consistent across the plant's filters for easier maintenance. The vote passed.

## Why this matters off the lake

Most Lake Mattoon property owners live outside Mattoon city limits and do not vote in these elections. But drainage decisions made here shape water moving through the watershed, and the meeting-with-a-resident commitment is a concrete thing to track.
