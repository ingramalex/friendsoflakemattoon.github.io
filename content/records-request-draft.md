# Draft records request

**To:** Susan J. O'Brien, City Clerk, City of Mattoon
208 North 19th Street, 2nd Floor, Mattoon, IL 61938 · 217-235-5655

Send by email if the Clerk's office gives you an address, otherwise by mail or
in person. **Do not wait for the City's FOIA form** — Illinois FOIA does not
require an agency's own form, and Mattoon's form happens to sit behind the same
CAPTCHA as the records themselves.

Two options below. Start with the first: it is a standing arrangement rather
than a records fight, and it is the one most likely to just work.

---

## Option 1 — Ask to be added to the distribution list (start here)

> Dear Ms. O'Brien,
>
> I'm writing on behalf of Friends of Lake Mattoon, a volunteer group of
> residents around Lake Mattoon.
>
> We publish a weekly summary for lake residents covering water quality,
> watershed planning, and upcoming meetings, and we'd like to make sure we're
> representing the Council's work accurately. Most of our members live outside
> city limits, so Council meetings are the main way we learn about decisions
> affecting the lake.
>
> Would it be possible to be added to a distribution list for City Council
> agendas, packets, and approved minutes as they are published? An email with
> the PDFs attached, or a link that doesn't require the download form, would be
> a great help.
>
> We're happy to work with whatever is easiest on your office. If a standing
> list isn't something you maintain, we'd be glad to submit a periodic FOIA
> request instead — I just wanted to ask the simpler way first.
>
> Thank you for your time,
>
> [name] · Friends of Lake Mattoon · [phone] · [email]

## Option 2 — Formal FOIA request (if Option 1 goes unanswered)

Illinois FOIA gives a public body **5 business days** to respond, extendable by
5 more with written notice (5 ILCS 140/3). Keep the scope narrow and the period
bounded — a broad standing request is easier to deny as unduly burdensome.

> Dear Ms. O'Brien,
>
> Under the Illinois Freedom of Information Act (5 ILCS 140), I request copies
> of the following records:
>
> 1. Approved minutes of all regular and special meetings of the Mattoon City
>    Council from [start date] to the present.
> 2. Agendas and meeting packets for the same period.
>
> I am requesting these records in electronic format (PDF), delivered by email
> to [email], which I understand carries no duplication fee.
>
> This request is made on behalf of Friends of Lake Mattoon for a
> non-commercial purpose: we publish a free public summary of lake and water
> quality matters for area residents.
>
> If any portion of this request is denied, please cite the specific exemption
> claimed and provide the remaining records.
>
> Sincerely,
>
> [name] · Friends of Lake Mattoon · [phone] · [email]

## Worth raising, gently and separately

Illinois OMA (5 ILCS 120/2.06) requires a public body that maintains a website
and has full-time staff to post approved open-meeting minutes to that website
within 10 days of approval, and to keep them posted for at least 60 days.

Mattoon does post them — but behind a CAPTCHA that also blocks screen readers
and any automated accessibility tooling, and which sits in front of the FOIA
request form as well.

That is worth mentioning as a usability problem rather than an accusation. Most
likely nobody chose it deliberately; it is a default in the WordPress download
plugin the site uses. A note to the Clerk or the City Manager pointing out that
the setting makes public records harder to reach — and that it also gates the
form people use to request records — has a decent chance of simply getting it
turned off.

If that happens, the automated brief can read agendas and minutes directly and
the manual step in `data/minutes/` goes away entirely.
