long it has gone unmentioned.

**Before you send any of this, watch the linked timestamps.** Every item below
came from an AI review of automatic captions, which mishear names, figures and
technical terms routinely. The timestamps are exact; the wording around them
may not be. One wrong number in a letter to the City costs you more credibility
than all ten of these items are worth.

**How the "no mention since" claims are phrased, and why.** We can only speak to
the eight meetings we reviewed. The City may well have acted outside a council
meeting — through staff, the Health Department, or a consultant — and we would
not see it. So every item asks for a status rather than alleging inaction. That
framing is also the one that survives a reply from the City Manager.

## 1. Septic systems around Lake Paradise and Lake Mattoon

**Ask:** On July 7 the City described working with the Coles County Health
Department to begin inspecting private sewage systems around Lake Paradise,
with coordination across county lines to follow. What is the status of those
inspections, how many have been completed, and has the coordination with Shelby
and Cumberland counties begun?

- Raised by a resident, then discussed by staff — [July 7 @ 03:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IkW22GlJH3Y&t=221s)
- Inter-county jurisdiction discussed — [July 7 @ 20:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IkW22GlJH3Y&t=1215s)
- No further mention in the 2 meetings since (40 days)

**Why it leads:** this is nutrient loading at the source, it affects the lake
itself rather than the treatment plant, and most of the shoreline sits in
counties the City does not govern. It is the clearest example of the lake
falling between jurisdictions.

## 3. The ozone trial — set the terms now, not in January

**Ask:** On July 21 the Council approved a lease with Water Tech Solutions for
a six-month ozone treatment trial. What are the criteria for judging it, who
decides whether to proceed, and will the results come back to Council in a
public session?

- Trial approved — [July 21 @ 08:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sq2_ZeawL4&t=520s)
- Discussion — [July 21 @ 09:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sq2_ZeawL4&t=575s)

**Why now, while nothing is overdue:** ozone treatment was one of exactly three
long-term fixes named in the City's own *Water Quality Response and Recovery
Report* after the July 2025 blooms. It is the first of the three to visibly
move. Asking for the success criteria at the start is what makes it possible to
ask a fair question in January; asking afterwards invites a moving target.

## 4. The recovery report's unfinished sections

**Ask:** The *Water Quality Response and Recovery Report* (August 2025)

- Report: https://mattoon.illinois.gov/wp-content/uploads/Water-Quality-Response-and-Recovery-Report.pdf

commitment to documenting not only what occurred, but also what was learned."

standard, not one imposed from outside — which is what makes it a fair question.

## Process asks — separate letter, separate tone

These are about access rather than the lake, and mixing them into the list
above would blunt it.

**Records behind a CAPTCHA.** Council agendas, packets and minutes are gated by
a CAPTCHA on the City site — as is the City's own FOIA request form. The
CAPTCHA also blocks screen readers and accessibility tooling. It is almost
certainly a default in the site's download plugin rather than a policy choice,
and switching it off would cost nothing.

**A distribution list.** Ask the City Clerk (Susan J. O'Brien, 217-235-5655) to
be added to a list for agendas, packets and approved minutes. Under Illinois
OMA (5 ILCS 120/2.06) approved minutes must be posted within 10 days of
approval and stay up 60 days — this is asking for the easy version of something
already required.
